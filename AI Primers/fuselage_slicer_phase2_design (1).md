## 1. Overview

This document describes the design and implementation plan for Phase 2 of the CuraEngine aircraft parts fork: a fuselage slicer that generates pseudo-geodesic internal structure for 3D printed RC aircraft and UAV fuselages.

Phase 1 delivered a rib-plane infill for single-wall aircraft wings, with procedural diagonal rib lines sweeping across the wing cross-section and spar pocket wall removal. Phase 2 extends this approach to fuselage construction using a helical stringer pattern inspired by Barnes Wallis's geodetic airframe principle.

### 1.1 Motivation

Conventional 3D printed fuselages use either solid infill (heavy), gyroid infill (lightweight but not load-path-optimised), or pure hollow shells (weak). None exploit the structural efficiency available from a semi-monocoque geodetic approach.

The Wellington bomber fuselage — built from two counter-winding helical families of load-bearing members — demonstrated that torsional loads in opposing spiral members cancel each other out, producing structures that remain functional even after significant localised damage. This document describes how to replicate that principle in FDM 3D printing by generating the helix families procedurally in the slicer rather than encoding them in the STL geometry.

### 1.2 Relationship to Phase 1

| | Phase 1 | Phase 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure generated** | Rib planes sweep along span axis | Helical stringers sweep along longitudinal axis |
| **Wall treatment** | Spar pocket wall removal | Inner cavity wall is STL-modelled; slicer does not modify it |
| **Input geometry** | Consistent wing profile | Arbitrary closed polygon per layer |
| **Shared** | CuraEngine 5.11 fork; same parameter exposure pattern; same build system ||

## 2. Structural Concept

### 2.1 Pseudo-Geodesic Semi-Monocoque

A true geodesic airframe requires members that follow great-circle paths on a curved surface and intersect such that torsional loads cancel. This implementation is **pseudo-geodesic** because:

- Members are straight chords in each print layer, not true geodesic curves on the fuselage surface
- The helix approximates geodesic behaviour by rotating the chord pattern incrementally with each layer
- No explicit transverse members are generated — the counter-rotating helix families serve as mutual transverse restraint at their crossing nodes

The result is structurally analogous to a geodetic frame but manufacturable on a standard FDM printer with no support material and no post-processing.

### 2.2 Load Path Logic

The outer single-wall skin carries hoop stress in tension. The radial walls of the corrugated stringer carry crushing and shear loads across the annulus. At the troughs, the stringer welds to the inboard face of the outer skin, bracing it against inward buckling — this is the primary mechanism by which the internal structure supports the skin. At the peaks, the stringer runs along the inner cavity wall, distributing load circumferentially at the inner boundary.

Where CW and CCW stringer families cross in the annulus, a structural node is formed that transfers load between families — this is the mechanism by which torsion resistance is achieved.

The inner cavity wall is **explicitly modelled in the STL** and is printed as a normal shell wall by CuraEngine without any modification. The slicer does not generate, suppress, or alter it. The stringer generated by Phase 2 occupies the annular space between the outer skin and this inner wall.

> **⚠ Critical constraint:** Hoop stiffness is low with a single-wall outer skin. Bulkhead layers at key Z positions (battery bay, wing tube socket, landing gear attachment) restore circumferential stiffness at load concentration points. See Section 6.5.

### 2.3 Comparison to Conventional Infill

| Strategy | Weight | Torsion | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solid infill | Heavy | Good | Not suitable for fuselages |
| Gyroid / lightning | Light | Isotropic | No preferred load path |
| Hollow shell only | Lightest | Poor | Buckles under point loads |
| **Phase 2 helical stringer** | **Light** | **Excellent** | **Structurally intentional** |

## 3. Geometry & Parameterisation

### 3.1 Coordinate System

All geometry is computed in **print space**. The longitudinal axis is Z (print direction). The fuselage is assumed to print upright (nose or tail up). Any print orientation is technically supported — the slicer operates relative to the per-layer centroid — but non-upright orientations produce non-intuitive stringer paths.

The reference origin at each layer is the **area-weighted centroid of the outer perimeter polygon**, not the bounding box centre. This correctly tracks asymmetric and tapered sections without user intervention.

### 3.2 Input Geometry — Two Polygon Modes

The fuselage STL is a **shell model** with both surfaces explicitly modelled in CAD. Depending on the Z station, each layer presents one of two cases:

#### Two-polygon layers (annular section)
The slicer sees **two closed polygons** — an outer skin perimeter and an inner cavity perimeter. The inner cavity is the hollow core of the fuselage, modelled directly into the STL. **The slicer does not generate the inner cavity wall.** It is treated as a pre-existing boundary that the stringer path uses as its inner rail.

The stringer lives entirely within the **annular region** between the two polygons.

#### Single-polygon layers (solid section)
At Z stations where the inner cavity has not yet begun or has ended (nose cone, tail cone, solid bulkhead zones), only the outer perimeter polygon is present. There is no inner boundary. In these zones the **bounding box centroid** acts as a degenerate inner boundary — a point rather than a curve. See Section 3.6.

### 3.3 Corrugation Cross-Section — The Square Wave Model

The stringer cross-section is a **square wave swept helically** around the annulus — alternating flat segments at two different radii (outer skin and inner perimeter), connected by short radial walls. One complete corrugation cycle has four segment types:

```
  outer skin ──── radial wall ──── inner perimeter ──── radial wall ──── outer skin
   (trough)         (rising)            (peak)            (falling)        (trough)
```

Zone boolean values (Section 4) determine which tangent segments run at the outer skin (trough) and which run at the inner perimeter (peak).

#### Trough segment — outer skin contact
Runs **parallel to and just inboard of the outer skin polygon**, offset inward by `skin_offset` (default = `nozzle_diameter`). Close enough to weld to the outer wall on the inboard face, bracing it against buckling, but not overlapping the outer wall toolpath.

#### Radial wall segment
Traverses radially across the full annulus depth from the outer skin offset to the inner perimeter (or centroid on single-polygon layers). The primary structural element — resists crushing and transfers shear between inner and outer boundaries. Both ends are trimmed inward by one line width to avoid overlapping the perimeter wall toolpaths.

#### Peak segment — inner perimeter arc
Runs **along the inner perimeter polygon**, connecting the inner endpoints of two adjacent radial walls. A true polygon-follow, not a chord approximation.

On **single-polygon layers**, the path reaches the centroid and immediately pivots to the next outbound radial wall — a V-shape rather than a U-shape. No inner arc segment is generated.

### 3.4 Stringer Placement — Relaxation Algorithm

Stringer positions are computed per layer using an iterative relaxation that distributes N radial walls to achieve visually uniform spacing on arbitrary cross-sections. Starting from equal angular spacing, the algorithm converges toward equal chord-distance spacing in three damped passes.

#### Step 1 — Layer centroid
Compute the area-weighted centroid C of the outer perimeter polygon at this layer.

#### Step 2 — Fixed stringer (helix 1, stringer 1)
Cast ray R[0] from C at angle:
```
θ[0] = phase_anchor_angle + layer_index × helix_pitch × layer_height
```
Clip to outer perimeter → point P[0]. This stringer does not move during relaxation.

#### Step 3 — Initial equal-angle distribution
Cast N−1 additional rays from C at `θ[k] = θ[0] + k × (360 / N)` for k = 1..N−1. Clip each to outer perimeter → P[1]..P[N−1].

#### Step 4 — Relaxation (3 iterations)
For iteration t = 1, 2, 3 with damping schedule a = [3.0, 2.0, 1.5]:

1. Compute chord distances: `D[k] = |P[(k+1) % N] − P[k]|` for k = 0..N−1
2. Compute mean: `D_avg = Σ D[k] / N`
3. For each non-fixed point P[k], k = 1..N−1:
   - Signed error: `err[k] = D[k−1] − D[k]`  (positive → move P[k] clockwise)
   - Maximum angular step: `δ_max = 360 / (a[t] × N)`
   - Rotate ray by `min(proportional correction, δ_max)` degrees, re-clip to outer perimeter

> **Note:** Movement is angular (rotating the ray from C), not Cartesian. Re-clipping after each step keeps the point on the actual perimeter polygon. The distance metric is linear chord length between adjacent perimeter points — not arc length along the polygon boundary. This produces visually uniform spacing on the cross-section view, which is the correct perceptual target.

#### Step 5 — Helix 2 stringer positions
Offset all helix 1 angles by `360 / (2N)` — half a sector. Apply independent relaxation. Helix 2 fixed stringer angle:
```
θ_H2[0] = phase_anchor_angle + 360/(2N) + layer_index × helix_pitch × layer_height
```

#### Step 6 — Inner perimeter projection
For each stringer in both helices, extend the ray inward to intersect the inner perimeter polygon (or centroid on single-polygon layers). This gives the near endpoint of each radial wall.

#### Step 7 — Endpoint trimming
Subtract one line width from both ends of each radial wall segment to avoid overlapping perimeter wall toolpaths.

### 3.5 Helix Pitch

Expressed as **degrees of rotation per mm of Z height**, independent of layer height.

- `360 / fuselage_length_mm` → one full helix cycle over the entire fuselage
- Higher pitch → more corrugation cycles per unit length, more skin contact points, tighter stringer crossing nodes
- Lower pitch → fewer cycles, longer tangent segments, lighter structure — subject to the FDM adhesion constraint in Section 5

### 3.6 Single-Polygon Layers — Centroid Mode

When no inner cavity polygon is present, the layer centroid C acts as a point inner boundary:

- The inner endpoint of every radial wall is C
- The path reaches C and **immediately pivots** to the next outbound radial wall — no inner arc, no dwell
- Result: V-shapes (two radial walls meeting at a point) rather than U-shapes

Detected automatically from polygon count at each layer. The transition zone where the cavity is growing from near-zero produces short inner arcs — geometrically correct, no special handling needed.

## 4. Zone & Sector Logic — Corrugation Routing

### 4.1 Definitions

**Sector S[n]** — the angular region between two adjacent stringers of a single helix. Helix 1 has N sectors; helix 2 has N sectors. Sectors are numbered clockwise from the fixed stringer of each helix.

Sector truth values **alternate** around the perimeter of each helix independently:
```
S[0] = true,  S[1] = false,  S[2] = true,  S[3] = false, ...
```

**Zone Z[n]** — the angular region between any two adjacent stringers from **either** helix. With two helices of N stringers each, there are 2N zones per layer. Zones are ordered clockwise from helix 1 stringer 1 (the fixed stringer) and recomputed each layer.

### 4.2 Zone Truth Value

Each zone Z[n] falls within exactly one sector from each helix. The truth value of the zone is resolved by applying the user-selected boolean operator to the two containing sector truth values:

```
Z[n] = OPERATOR( H1.S[containing(n)],  H2.S[containing(n)] )
```

#### OR — dense coverage
`Z[n] = H1.S[k] | H2.S[j]`

Fires wherever either helix's sector is true. Since the two helices are offset by half a period, OR produces near-continuous outer-skin contact with only narrow inner-perimeter windows. Conservative — maximum adhesion, heavier structure. Useful for large-diameter fuselages or as a verification mode.

#### XOR — alternating basket-weave (default)
`Z[n] = H1.S[k] ^ H2.S[j]`

Fires where exactly one helix's sector is true. Produces a clean alternating pattern — each helix owns alternating zones around the perimeter, interleaving like a basket weave. Minimum weight, structurally honest, the correct approximation of true geodetic alternating attachment.

#### AND and NAND — not implemented
AND fires only where both sectors coincide (near-never, given the half-period offset) — degenerate sparse structure. NAND fires almost everywhere — degenerates to a near-solid ring. Neither is useful; neither is implemented.

| Operator | Formula | Outer skin zones | Inner perimeter zones | Implemented |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OR | `H1\|H2` | ~75% | ~25% | ✅ |
| XOR | `H1^H2` | ~50% | ~50% | ✅ |
| AND | `H1&H2` | ~0% | ~100% | ❌ |
| NAND | `!(H1&H2)` | ~100% | ~0% | ❌ |

### 4.3 Tangent Geometry Routing

Once zone truth values are resolved, the corrugation path is built by walking the 2N zones clockwise:

- **Z[n] = true** → tangent segment runs at the **far end** of the bounding stringers (outer perimeter), offset inboard by `skin_offset`. Connects the outer endpoints of the leading and trailing radial walls, following the outer perimeter polygon.

- **Z[n] = false** → tangent segment runs at the **near end** (inner perimeter), offset outboard by `skin_offset`. Connects the inner endpoints of the leading and trailing radial walls, following the inner perimeter polygon.

At each stringer boundary, the path traverses radially between the current tangent level and the next — the radial wall segment.

The result for each helix is one **continuous serpentine polyline** per layer. Both helices output as separate toolpaths; their physical intersection points in the annular space fuse on contact.

### 4.4 Stringer Overlap and Deletion

As the helix pitch rotates the two stringer families relative to each other along Z, individual H1 and H2 stringers may approach one another. When two stringers from opposing helices are too close to print without overlapping extrusions, both are deleted from that layer and the surrounding zones merge into a zero-width cusp.

#### Overlap detection

For each H1/H2 stringer pair, compute angular separation at the current layer. A collision is flagged when:

**Condition A — coincident:** Angular separation < `δ_min` at current layer.

**Condition B — closest approach:** Angular separation at current layer is less than at both the previous and next layer (local minimum) AND separation < `δ_min`.

The collision threshold is:
```
δ_min = arctan( line_width / radial_depth )
```
where `radial_depth` is the annulus depth at that angular position. This is the angle subtended by one line width at the outer perimeter — the minimum separation at which the two extrusion paths would overlap.

> **Note:** True closest-approach detection (checking all three layers per pair) is O(N) per layer and cheap. The `arctan` threshold avoids per-layer distance integration while remaining physically meaningful.

#### Deletion behaviour

Both stringers in a colliding pair are removed from the merged zone list for that layer only. The two zones adjacent to the deleted pair — Z[n-1] and Z[n+1] — now share a boundary at the **midpoint** of the deleted stringer pair's angular positions. This is a **cusp point**: both tangent segments terminate here, the zone between them has zero angular width, and no tangent segment is generated for it. The path from Z[n-1] and the path from Z[n+1] simply meet at the cusp.

On the layer above and below, both stringers are present at their normal positions. The deletion is exactly one layer thick.

```
Normal layer:   ... ── Z[n-1] ──●S_H1●── Z[n] ──●S_H2●── Z[n+1] ── ...
Deleted layer:  ... ── Z[n-1] ──────────── ╳ ──────────── Z[n+1] ── ...
                                          cusp (zero-width zone)
```

### 4.5 Crossing Nodes

Where H1 and H2 stringer toolpaths physically intersect in the annular space, the two extruded lines touch and fuse — forming a passive structural node. No special toolpath generation is needed; the crossing is a natural consequence of both helices being printed on the same layer.

> **Future work:** Active node reinforcement — detect the 2D intersection point of H1 and H2 radial walls within the annulus and emit a brief overextrusion pulse to form an explicit thickened node (`node_reinforce` parameter).

## 5. FDM Pitch Adhesion Constraint

### 5.1 The Problem

FDM requires that adjacent extruded lines be close enough to fuse — typically within **1.5× the nozzle diameter** laterally. For helical stringers, each layer's chord lines are rotated slightly relative to the previous layer's. If the pitch is too slow (rotation per layer too small), consecutive layers' chord lines are nearly parallel and nearly collocated, but do not share endpoints — they print into air and fail to adhere.

Counterintuitively, **too slow a pitch breaks adhesion** — the lines are close in angle but miss each other laterally just enough to not fuse. This is the dominant failure mode for this structure on an FDM printer.

### 5.2 Minimum Pitch Formula

For an outer skin arc segment of length `L` (the trough segment between two adjacent radial walls) and a per-layer rotation of `θ` degrees, the lateral displacement of that segment between adjacent layers is:

```
d ≈ L × sin(θ_per_layer)  ≈  L × θ_per_layer × (π / 180)    [for small θ]
```

For adhesion: `d ≤ k × nozzle_diameter`, where `k ≈ 1.5` (empirical FDM fusion threshold).

Rearranging:

```
θ_min_per_layer  =  arcsin( k × nozzle_diameter / L )  ×  (180 / π)

pitch_min (°/mm)  =  θ_min_per_layer / layer_height
```

Where:
- `L` = outer arc segment length at this layer (outer perimeter / spoke_count, approximately)
- `nozzle_diameter` = from Cura machine settings
- `layer_height` = from Cura print settings
- `k` = 1.5 (hardcoded default, exposed as `adhesion_factor` for advanced users)

### 5.3 Arc Segment Length Estimation

The outer skin arc segment length between adjacent radial walls is approximately:

```
L_approx  ≈  outer_perimeter / spoke_count
```

For arbitrary polygons, the actual minimum arc segment length is computed per layer from the arc-length sampled point set (the shortest gap between any two adjacent sample points). The slicer uses this minimum as the conservative estimate for the adhesion check.

### 5.4 Taper Interaction

On a tapered fuselage, chord length decreases toward the narrow end. This **tightens** the minimum pitch constraint toward the tail — the same `helix_pitch` setting that satisfies adhesion at the widest fuselage section may violate it at the tail. 

The validation pass (Section 8.2) checks the pitch constraint at every layer and warns at the first layer where it is violated, so the user knows exactly which fuselage station is the limiting constraint.

### 5.5 Maximum Pitch

There is also a practical upper bound: too fast a pitch produces chord crossings with a wide angular separation, and the unsupported span between crossing nodes grows. When that span exceeds the printer's bridging capability (~40–60 mm for most setups), interior chord lines sag.

```
pitch_max (°/mm)  =  arcsin( bridge_span_max / L ) × (180/π) / layer_height
```

This is a softer constraint than the minimum — sagging chords are weak but the print does not fail catastrophically. It is flagged as a warning, not an error.

### 5.6 Validation Behaviour

| Condition | Severity | Action |
|---|---|---|
| `pitch < pitch_min` at any layer | **Error** | Warn with first violating layer Z and the minimum required pitch at that station |
| `pitch > pitch_max` at any layer | Warning | Warn with first violating layer Z and estimated bridge span |
| Pitch within bounds at all layers | OK | No output |

## 6. Implementation Plan

### 6.1 CuraEngine Integration Points

Phase 2 follows the same architectural pattern as Phase 1, modifying CuraEngine's infill generation path rather than the slicing or wall generation stages.

A new infill pattern type `FuselageGeodesic` is added alongside the existing rib pattern, selected via `fuselage_enable`. When active for a given layer, standard infill generation is skipped and the corrugated stringer path is generated and injected in its place. The outer skin wall and inner cavity wall (where present) are printed normally by CuraEngine — the slicer does not alter wall generation for either boundary.

The key inputs to the stringer generator at each layer are:
- **Outer polygon** — the outer skin perimeter, from CuraEngine's normal wall detection
- **Inner polygon** — the inner cavity perimeter if present, detected as a second closed polygon inside the outer at this layer; `null` if absent (single-polygon layer)

### 6.2 Modified Files

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `src/infill.cpp` | Add `FuselageGeodesic` case; perimeter sampling; corrugation path generation; arc assignment operator |
| `src/FffGcodeWriter.cpp` | Inner/outer polygon detection per layer; bulkhead layer detection |
| `include/settings/types/` | New enum types if needed |
| `dist/fdmprinter.def.json` | Parameter exposure to Cura UI |
| `dist/install.ps1` | Updated installer |

### 6.3 Zone and Sector Implementation

Sector truth values are assigned by index parity within each helix. Zone truth values are resolved per the selected operator. S1 and S2 are computed independently before the operator is applied, keeping the geometry debuggable.

```cpp
// Assign sector truth values (alternating per helix)
auto sectorTruth = [](int sector_index) -> bool {
    return (sector_index % 2) == 0;
};

// For each zone in the merged clockwise list:
for (int z = 0; z < zone_count; ++z) {
    int h1_sector = h1SectorContaining(z);   // which H1 sector this zone falls in
    int h2_sector = h2SectorContaining(z);   // which H2 sector this zone falls in
    bool s1 = sectorTruth(h1_sector);
    bool s2 = sectorTruth(h2_sector);

    bool zone_val;
    switch (arc_mode) {
        case ArcMode::OR:  zone_val = s1 | s2; break;
        case ArcMode::XOR: zone_val = s1 ^ s2; break;
    }
    zone_values[z] = zone_val;
}

// Overlap / deletion scan
float delta_min = std::atan(line_width / radial_depth) * (180.0f / M_PI);
for each H1/H2 stringer pair (i, j):
    float sep_prev = angularSeparation(i, j, layer - 1);
    float sep_curr = angularSeparation(i, j, layer);
    float sep_next = angularSeparation(i, j, layer + 1);
    bool closest_approach = (sep_curr < sep_prev) && (sep_curr < sep_next);
    if (sep_curr < delta_min || closest_approach && sep_curr < delta_min):
        markDeleted(i); markDeleted(j);
        cuspPoints.push_back( midAngle(i, j) );
```

### 6.4 Pitch Validation Implementation

The pitch check runs once per layer before stringer generation. The relevant span length `L` is the **outer skin arc segment length** between two adjacent radial walls — this is the trough segment that must fuse to its counterpart on the adjacent layer. It uses the minimum arc segment length across the current layer:

```cpp
float L_min = *std::min_element(outer_arc_lengths.begin(), outer_arc_lengths.end());
float theta_min = std::asin(adhesion_factor * nozzle_diameter / L_min) * (180.0f / M_PI);
float pitch_min = theta_min / layer_height;

if (helix_pitch < pitch_min) {
    log_warning("Fuselage slicer: pitch %.2f °/mm below adhesion minimum %.2f °/mm at Z=%.2f mm "
                "(min arc segment %.2f mm). Increase helix_pitch or reduce spoke_count.",
                helix_pitch, pitch_min, layer_z, L_min);
}
```

### 6.5 Bulkhead Layers

At Z heights in `bulkhead_positions`, the fuselage slicer substitutes a near-solid concentric ring:

- Detect when current layer Z is within one layer height of a bulkhead position
- Generate concentric infill at `bulkhead_density` (default 95%) for that layer only
- Resume helical generation on the next layer with phase continuity preserved

> **Note:** Bulkhead positions are not auto-detected. They must be set manually at wing tube sockets, battery bay ends, motor bulkhead, and landing gear attach points.

### 6.6 Dual Helix Family

When `dual_helix` is enabled (default: `true`), two passes of chord generation run per layer — CW and CCW. The CCW family is offset by N/2 spokes from the CW family, producing crossing nodes at roughly the geometric midpoints of the CW chords.

When disabled, only the CW family is generated. Lighter and faster to print, but no torsional node network — suitable only for very light fuselages or tail booms.

| `bulkhead_density` | `95` | int % | Infill density at bulkhead layers |

## 8. Model Constraints & Validation

### 8.1 Required Model Properties

| Constraint | Effect if violated |
|---|---|
| Fuselage prints upright | Non-upright prints produce non-intuitive stringer paths (not prevented) |
| Simple closed polygon at every layer | Holes or self-intersections cause degenerate chord generation |
| Cross-section evolves gradually along Z | Abrupt steps cause phase discontinuities |
| Centroid lateral drift < 10% of section diameter per layer | Larger drift causes stringer paths to wander unpredictably |

### 8.2 Validation Pass

Runs once before infill generation. Emits warnings to CuraEngine's standard log — does not halt slicing.

| Check | Threshold | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Centroid lateral drift | > 10% of local section diameter between adjacent layers | Warning |
| Cross-section area change | > 40% between adjacent layers | Warning |
| Tangent segment length vs nozzle | Min tangent segment < 3× nozzle diameter (corrugation too fine to print) | Warning |
| Pitch vs adhesion minimum | `helix_pitch < pitch_min` at any layer | **Error-level warning** |
| Pitch vs bridge maximum | `helix_pitch > pitch_max` at any layer | Warning |
| Stringer overlap | Angular separation < `δ_min` at any H1/H2 pair | Info — triggers deletion and cusp |
| Single-polygon transition | Inner cavity polygon appears or disappears between adjacent layers | Info — triggers centroid mode |
